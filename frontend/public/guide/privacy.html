Privacy statement

Nisaba is a desktop inventory-sync manager: it keeps one canonical catalog of your products on your own machine and synchronizes quantities and listings with the marketplaces you sell on. There is no Nisaba cloud account, no Nisaba server that receives your data, no telemetry, and no crash-reporting service. This page says exactly what leaves your machine, and what never does.

What leaves the machine

Marketplace API calls — only to platforms you connected

When you connect a platform and run a sync cycle, Nisaba sends requests to that platform's own servers. What travels is your own catalog and listing data — products, variants, prices, quantities, photos — and it goes only to the platform it belongs to. Marketplace requests never pass through any Nisaba-operated infrastructure, because there isn't any.

  • eBay (HTTPS, OAuth): your listings, offers, inventory quantities, and category/business-policy lookups. Nisaba only asks for inventory and account-metadata scopes — it never requests order or buyer data.
  • Amazon (HTTPS, login-with-Amazon OAuth): your listings and quantity/price updates. Nisaba never calls the Orders API and never requests restricted buyer data.
  • Squarespace (HTTPS, your own API key): your products and inventory adjustments.
  • XMR Bazaar (HTTPS, your own account): your own listings, inventory fields, and sales-page reads — exactly the pages your logged-in account would open in a browser.

Your credentials are stored in the OS keyring and are sent only to that platform's own login/token endpoints. They are never sent to any third party.

Update checks — GitHub, and you can turn them off

The app checks for updates at launch and then every 6 hours. Each check is a single HTTPS request to the GitHub-hosted release feed. GitHub sees what any HTTPS request reveals — your IP address, the app version, the OS — and nothing about your catalog, credentials, or sync activity.

You can turn automatic checks off in Settings. A manual "Check for updates" is your own explicit action and is not gated by the setting.

P2P company sync — only if you enable it, over Tor

Syncing between your own installs is off by default. When you enable it, traffic runs over Tor onion services, which hides each peer's IP from the other peers and from network observers, and payloads are encrypted peer-to-peer (AES-GCM). What observers of the network can see is only what any onion-service traffic reveals — timing and circuit metadata — not content.

Vendor plugins — only hosts you allow

Vendor plugins (like a supplier catalog importer) run in a sandbox. A plugin can only reach the network through the hosts it declared when you installed it; you approve each plugin and its hosts, and private-network addresses are always refused.

What never leaves the machine

  • No telemetry, no analytics calls home. The analytics features are computed locally from your own database. No usage statistics, no crash reports, no phone-home of any kind.
  • Your full catalog and local database stay on your machine. They are only pushed, listing by listing, to the marketplaces you configured.
  • API keys and OAuth tokens live in the OS keyring and travel only to their own platform's endpoints.
  • Order and buyer data are not read at all. None of the adapters reads order APIs with buyer details today, so no buyer data is collected, stored, transmitted, or shared anywhere. If a future version ever reads order data, this statement gets updated first.

Your keys, your terms

Nisaba runs on your own developer credentials, not a shared keyset. That means you accept each platform's developer terms when you create the keys — see the eBay account-deletion page and the credentials page for the steps each platform asks of you — and you are responsible for keeping your machine's keyring and backups safe.
