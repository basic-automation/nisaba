Mappings and variants

The catalog model

Nisaba keeps one canonical catalog per company:

  • Products — your master records: SKU, title, description, images, and the canonical quantity.
  • Listings — the per-platform copies of a product (an eBay listing, an Amazon offer, a Squarespace product, an XMR Bazaar post). A listing points back to its product.
  • Variants — size/color/style children of a product (e.g. "T-shirt / Red / L"). Each variant has its own attributes and quantity, and each variant can have its own listings per platform.

How mapping works

  1. Automatic matching on import. When a platform's listings are first pulled in, Nisaba tries to match each listing to an existing product — by SKU first, then by title similarity. Matches become links; anything unmatched becomes a new product (or a new variant under an existing product when the platform reports parent/child relationships).
  2. Review the links. Open a product and check its Listings tab. If a listing landed on the wrong product, unlink it and link the right one — the link is what tells sync "these two are the same thing".
  3. Keep one link per platform per variant. A variant should have at most one listing on each platform. Two eBay listings linked to the same variant will fight over the quantity on every sync.

Variants across platforms

Platforms describe variants differently — eBay uses item specifics, Amazon uses variation themes, Squarespace uses product variants. Nisaba normalizes these into variant attributes (key/value pairs like color=Red, size=L) on its own variant records.

  • Sync snapshots are tracked per (product, platform, variant), so a sale of "Red / L" on eBay changes only that variant's quantity, not the whole product family.
  • If a platform's variant structure doesn't line up with yours (e.g. the platform merged two sizes into one listing), fix the mapping on the product rather than editing quantities — quantity edits get overwritten by the next sync.
  • Adding a brand-new variant on one platform and linking it into the family propagates it as a new listing to the other enabled platforms on the next sync that creates listings.

Map first, sync second

The single most common source of "sync did something weird" is a wrong or missing mapping. Before your first real sync cycle, run a dry run and scan it for listings pushing changes to products you didn't expect — that's a mapping to fix, not a sync bug.
